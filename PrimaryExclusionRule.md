# Property sale -- primary exclusion rule (US+MN))

## Primary Residence Exclusion Rule

The primary residence exclusion allows you to exclude a significant amount of profit from the sale of your main home from capital gains taxes. Here are the key details:

### Exclusion Amounts

For single filers, up to $250,000 of capital gains can be excluded, while married couples filing jointly can exclude up to $500,000.

### Eligibility Requirements

To qualify for this exclusion, you must meet these criteria:

1. **Ownership Test**: You must have owned the home
2. **Use Test**: You must have used the home as your principal (primary) residence  
3. **Time Test**: You've lived in the home for at least two of the past five years before the sale.

### What This Means

If you bought a home for $350,000 and sold it for $550,000, your capital gain is $200,000. Since this is under the $250,000 limit (for single filers), you would owe **$0 in capital gains taxes** on that sale.

### Timing Consideration

You haven't claimed the exclusion on another home in the past two years. So you can only use this exclusion once every two years.

### Impact for Most Homeowners

Many property sales don't net more than these maximums. So, many home sellers in Minnesota and western Wisconsin don't end up paying taxes on the gains they earned from selling.

**Bottom line**: Unless you made more than $250,000 (or $500,000 if married filing jointly) in profit on your home sale, you likely won't owe any capital gains tax on it.
